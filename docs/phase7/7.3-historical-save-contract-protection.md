# Phase 7.3 - Historical Save Contract Protection

Implemented 2026-11-08. Schema **22 is unchanged**. This increment isolates historical interpretation; it adds no playable content, room migration, expansion credit, refund, price change or reward change. All changes remain uncommitted. Existing Phase 6/7.1/7.2 working-tree changes were retained.

## Root causes and dependency inventory

The economy reader is `src/persistence/game-save.ts`; there is no `economy-save.ts` in this project. The chain runs journey 1-12 -> economy 13 -> museum 14 -> travel 15 -> tickets 16 -> helpers 17 -> custody 18 -> upgrades 19 -> deposits 20 -> pending cash 21 -> ledger 22. Schema 22 also uses lower readers and shared field validators directly for financial reconciliation.

| Reader or migration | Previous live dependency | Compatibility failure | Protection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `save.ts` | Part catalogs, discovery/cleaning settings, legacy and current sites, burial exposure | New parts could invalidate closed sites; changed footprints/depths could erase accepted exposure or invalidate inventory | Frozen IDs, required sets, masks, cell IDs, depths and exposure fractions for each historical layout |
| `game-save.ts` | Live sites, economy settings, `soilUnits`, `coinOutcome`, `createGameSave` during migration | Changed rewards or soil keys could reject saved manifests; migration could introduce future soil/rewards | Frozen economy v1 hash, reward parameters, layout identity, units and migration manifest creation |
| `museum-save.ts`, `migrateMuseumSave` | Live `PURCHASES`, prices and length; live display/stand checks | New offers could be granted free, invalidate complete legacy grants, or change receipt/prerequisite interpretation | Four historical offers and prices, fixed grants and three historical exhibit/stand relationships |
| `travel-save.ts`, `migrateTravelSave` | Live map prices, site IDs/cells, display/ownership/completion helpers | Changed unlocks or layouts could remove earned access, invalidate payment or alter interrupted-exit restoration | Frozen two-site access, 24-coin receipt and historical completion rules |
| `ticket-save.ts`, `ledger-save.ts` | Live capacity/queue limits and earned ticket price through live exhibits | New display requirements or tuning could invalidate paid history and visit sequences | Frozen capacity 6, queue 3 and historical display-price calculation; ledger arithmetic unchanged |
| `helper-save.ts` | Live hire price/availability, displays, sites and `validHelperTask` | Existing receipts could fail; purchased helper assignments/work could be retired under changed layouts/speeds | Frozen 60-coin receipt, one available helper, original prerequisites and base work units |
| `storage-save.ts` | Live discovery exposure, origin sites, ownership and helper task checks | Accepted stored/delivering finds could become invalid or work could be lost | Frozen origin/exposure/task validation; existing delivery recovery unchanged |
| `upgrade-save.ts` | Live upgrade IDs/prices and displays | Existing receipts or eligibility could become invalid | Frozen 16/24/30 prices and original prerequisites |
| `payment-save.ts` | Live purchase prices and `purchaseStatus` | Partial deposits could become overpaid, newly eligible, or invalid due to catalog/prerequisite changes | Frozen offer/prerequisite checks; existing reconciliation unchanged |
| `cash-save.ts` | Indirect lower-reader dependencies | Pending/spendable reconciliation could fail after lower rules changed | Protected through the lower readers; this file required no change |

## Contracts and scope

`src/persistence/contracts/journey-v12.ts` contains only historical validation data: 13 part/discovery identities, the 336 cleaning indices and original eligible mask, eight site-layout definitions used by the schema chain, required discovery sets and prerequisites. Regular 8-by-8 grids generate stable row-major cell identities. Explicit cell exposure fractions preserve the two-layer Eryops spine, including its shallow segment. These are literal historical definitions, not snapshots cloned from live catalogs at import time. Labels, art, world positions, meshes, paths and camera data are absent.

`economy-v1.ts` pins starting coins 20, layout revision 1, one coin at probability 0.125, the existing integer hash and `/v1/` cell/layer keys. Pre-economy migration excludes already-cleared soil and uses the supplied seed exactly as before. Existing manifests are validated, not regenerated or repaired. Claims and exclusions retain their original accounting and duplicate checks.

`progression-v22.ts` pins only ownership, receipt, prerequisite, display-price, site-access and helper-work rules needed by schemas 14-22. The frozen museum catalog is:

| ID | Purchased amount | Legacy amount |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `stand-dimetrodon` | 12 | 0 |
| `stand-trilobite` | 8 | 0 |
| `west-wing` | 18 | 0 |
| `stand-eryops` | 12 | 0 |

Pre-museum migrations grant precisely these four legacy entries. A historical legacy hall remains an all-four, zero-paid grant; catalog growth cannot change its meaning. Purchased west-wing ownership stays `source: purchase, paid: 18`. Partial contributions remain integer amounts 1-17, subject to the original prerequisites. No credit field, refund or spendable currency adjustment is introduced.

All contract data is recursively frozen. Runtime imports in these modules reference only other historical contracts; game imports are type-only. The existing public `migrateMuseumSave` entry point uses the frozen four-offer list. `migrateTravelSave` delegates to the frozen migration. Ordinary purchasing, travel, digging, helpers, ticket service and constructors continue to use live gameplay definitions.

## Rules deliberately shared

The reader chain, envelope shapes, strict field checks, integer reconciliation and writer/committer were retained. These are stable structural rules, not catalog-driven behavior. The following shared game helpers remain safe **under their current implementations**:

- Empty-envelope migrations for tickets, helpers, custody, upgrades, deposits and pending cash; they add fixed fields without consulting content.
- `migrateLedgerSave`: maps three historical counters to price keys 2/3/4 without changing income.
- `ledgerTotals`, `validTicketPrice`, and `ticketIncome`: canonical numeric keys, integer bounds and arithmetic; no catalog lookup. The ledger sub-contract remains version 1.
- `retirePaid`: removes already-paid transient visits without paying them again.
- `settleDeliveries`: changes delivery custody to storage at the saved origin site; it does not query live sites or grant collection/rewards.

Changing any of these semantics later requires compatibility review. Their modules also contain gameplay code; static/transitive imports alone do not imply that historical validation executes that gameplay code.

## Verified compatibility guarantees

The original nine Phase 7.1 JSON fixtures remain untouched. All 45 baseline tests and all 69 Phase 7.2 ledger tests pass in the full suite.

Nine new tests in `historical-contracts.test.ts` use the same fixtures plus minimal inputs for every schema 1-21, a fully closed two-site economy snapshot, an interrupted helper task, and migrated schema-22 copies. Six separate child processes mutate live catalogs **before importing any reader**. The worker changes purchases/prices/kinds, exhibit parts and cleaning settings, current and prior site layouts/IDs/depths/prerequisites, map costs, deterministic reward settings, helper tuning, upgrade prices and visitor limits/prices. It also runs all changes together. Production catalog files are never edited for these scenarios.

Each scenario compares **79 results**: 64 valid inputs and 15 invalid/tampered inputs. Accepted results are compared in full with the unmodified-catalog results, then round-tripped and loaded through a read-only storage adapter. This establishes for the exercised inputs:

- Stable legacy grants even when future stands/rooms are appended; direct schema-13 museum migration grants only the historical four.
- Unchanged balances, pending revenue, paid receipts, deposits, ticket counts and locked paid visits.
- Stable site closures, collected/placed fossils, custody and partial helper work despite altered live content.
- Identical deterministic soil outcomes, claims and exclusions, including migration from pre-economy saves.
- Continued rejection of inflated/negative money, future/duplicate/altered receipts, unknown/overpaid deposits, unearned closure, unknown collected finds, unknown soil, altered layout/outcomes and duplicate claims.

Other new tests verify full schema-22 round trips, exact west-wing paid/legacy/deposit examples, deep immutability, absence of runtime game/content imports in the contracts, and every historical burial footprint immediately at and below its exposure boundary. The boundary comparison intentionally compares against the current baseline; future intentional live-layout changes must retain the frozen expectations and revise that comparison deliberately.

Existing failed-write, duplicate-action, retry, reload and reset tests remain intact. Browser regressions additionally verify real application migration/retry, helper work and custody recovery.

## Schema 22 behavior and remaining dependencies

The serialized schema, ledger representation and financial equations are unchanged. Reading existing supported saves now uses fixed content semantics. Parsing preserves paid visits; loading still retires them. Loading still settles interrupted deliveries and pauses valid helper tasks. Those are existing recovery rules, not newly invented loss of progress.

**This does not make arbitrary future content writable under schema 22.** Unknown room/stand/site/fossil IDs remain rejected; earned historical ticket tiers still derive from the three historical displays. Generic ledger arithmetic supports higher prices, but a future full-save reader must validate the progression that earned them.

Known remaining live dependencies and limits:

1. New-game constructors and absent-storage branches intentionally use live gameplay content. They are not historical-save migrations. Future additions need a matching new writer/reader contract before release.
2. After load, ordinary gameplay/rendering uses live site, burial, navigation, exhibit and purchase definitions. This task proves historical validation/migration isolation, not that arbitrary edits to old playable sites can safely resume an old game. Preserve existing site IDs/layouts or explicitly migrate/map them before changing gameplay interpretation. Added new site IDs must not be silently mixed into historical manifests.
3. Historical types still reuse game interfaces through type-only imports. Structural changes may require explicit historical types in a later phase; they cannot silently change runtime contract constants.
4. Shared pure helper implementations listed above remain maintenance dependencies. Their semantics must stay fixed or be versioned if future features change them.
5. Future removal/redistribution of displays can conflict with earned ticket history unless the new migration preserves earned rights or explicitly changes the validation model.

No real 17-exhibit catalog, three-room progression, new site gameplay or expansion-credit migration exists in this change. Those integrations are untested and are not claimed as completed.

## Independent engineering assessment and Phase 7.4 recommendations

- **Verified, no Critical defect found in the bounded work:** the highest-risk historical catalog dependencies are isolated and tested before module import. Existing prices, grants, reward interpretation, financial records and schema 22 are preserved under the exercised mutations.
- **High, future integration requirement:** room/purchase/site definitions can be added without contaminating these old readers, but cannot safely be shipped as playable persisted content merely by editing the catalogs. Define the next full-save contract, validate old saves first, then explicitly map them into the new world. Keep historical contracts closed to future additions.
- **High, future excavation risk:** live gameplay still interprets loaded progress. Preserve historical playable site identities/layouts or provide an explicit runtime mapping. Add a browser continuation test that loads each old site, performs one action, saves, reloads and verifies closure/manifests/currency after actual new sites arrive.
- **Medium, west-wing credit migration:** the necessary evidence is retained: purchased 18, partial 1-17 and legacy paid 0. Credit eligibility, destination, consumption order and treatment of partial contributions still need the future migration policy. Derive credit from recorded investment, never ownership alone; test migration interruption, duplicate retry, partial/full consumption and reload so neither spendable coins nor credit can be granted twice. No credit is granted now.
- **Medium, earned ticket history:** preserve old earned revenue tiers when rooms/displays are redistributed. Test full saves with actual future exhibit counts; generic ledger tests alone cannot establish that integration.
- **Low, maintenance:** frozen rule copies can drift if later edited as ordinary tuning. Treat them as versioned history, retain the baseline fixtures, and keep the subprocess isolation tests. Review shared pure helpers when changing their semantics. The build-size warning is unchanged and outside this task.

All bounded Phase 7.3 implementation and validation work is complete. Future-world and credit integration are recommendations for subsequent phases, not claims of compatibility already proved here.
