# Phase 7.2 — Extensible ticket revenue ledger

Implemented 2026-11-08. Scope: ticket accounting and compatible persistence only. No exhibits, rooms, sites, admission prices, arrival intervals, cashier interactions, geometry or west-wing credit behavior changed. The Phase 7.1 JSON fixtures and baseline test file remain unchanged.

## Root cause and representation

The inspected schema-21 `CashSave` inherited `TicketSave.visitors.sales: [number, number, number]`. `payVisitor` indexed that array by `price - 2`; `parseTicketSave` required exactly three counters and paid amounts in `{0, 2, 3, 4}`. With the existing `1 + completedDisplayCount` price formula, four displays would select index 3, producing invalid accounting. Arrival timing separately uses the existing 12/10/8-second schedule with its final interval capped; that behavior is preserved.

Normal play now uses **schema 22 (`LedgerSave`)**, with this replacement for `visitors.sales`:

```json
{ "version": 1, "counts": { "2": 3, "3": 2, "4": 1, "18": 1 } }
```

The example describes ledger structure, not a currently obtainable museum. Keys are canonical decimal integer prices of at least 2; counts are non-negative safe integers. Missing prices mean zero sales. New saves start with empty `counts`; migration omits old zero counters. Explicit zero counters are accepted and confer no income or tier entitlement. There is no array length or content-sized price cap. Safe-integer bounds apply to prices, counts, multiplication, accumulated income and reconciliation.

`ticketPriceForDisplays` retains the current formula. `payVisitor` still derives price from completed, owned displays and checks museum location, cashier proximity, attendance and the first unpaid visitor. For schema 22 it delegates the accounting step to `payLedgerTicket`; this adds the price to pending revenue and updates that visit and its ledger counter together. The helper accepts an explicit price so its pure accounting can be tested independently of fossil content; production always supplies the derived price. Historical transitions retain their original array representation and cannot write a fourth tier.

## Reader and migration path

`src/app/prototype.ts` uses `createLedgerSave` / `loadLedgerSave` for initialization, loading and reset. The save key, isolated QA adapters, exclusive writer lock, committer and save writer are unchanged.

For schemas **1–21**, `parseLedgerSave` first invokes the existing schema-21 reader and historical migration chain. Only a validated result is converted: array indices 0/1/2 become price keys `"2"`/`"3"`/`"4"`. Coins, pending revenue, visit IDs and paid prices, receipts, partial contributions, finds, placements, soil claims, sites, helpers, custody and upgrades are copied without grants. Earlier pre-pending saves retain their already-spendable ticket income and migrate with pending revenue zero, as before.

Parsing preserves paid visitor records and their locked prices. Loading retains the established recovery behavior: retire paid tours, keep unpaid visitors, pause/recover helper work and settle delivery finds into storage at their original site. This does not repay tickets or collect fossils. The application commits the migrated/recovered snapshot before enabling gameplay; a failed write retains the old durable save and retries the same candidate.

For schema **22**, the reader validates the new ledger and visitor state directly. It reuses extracted helper/upgrade/deposit/custody field validators, and the existing travel/museum/economy reconciliation with independently calculated ticket income. Validated post-travel spending is temporarily refunded for that accounting check, then the actual spendable balance is restored. It does **not** translate new sales into invented three-tier historical records or skip progression checks. Historical array validators remain strict; schemas 16–21 do not accept the new ledger or extra historical tiers. The small field-validator extractions preserve their prior rules.

The full reader derives the permitted positive-sales maximum from validated completed displays. Today, a full save claiming 18-coin sales without the corresponding museum progress is rejected. Once additional exhibits exist, the same ledger and ticket-validator structure can represent their derived prices without another sales-schema change.

## Financial and duplicate protections

The conserved equation remains:

`coins + pending tickets + completed spending + partial deposits = starting currency + claimed soil rewards + lifetime ticket income`

Lifetime ticket income is now `sum(price × sales count)`. Validated museum/site/helper/upgrade receipts and deposits are included exactly once; legacy ownership still contributes zero paid value. West-wing receipts and contributions retain their original meaning.

Checks include canonical price keys; safe, non-negative counts; exact products/sums; supported ledger version/shape; maximum active/unpaid visitors; increasing unique visit IDs; `sold + unpaid = nextId - 1`; and active paid visits backed by lifetime sales **at their recorded price**. Pending cash cannot exceed lifetime ticket income. The schema-22 reader checks total accounted assets for overflow. Payment also checks the conserved lifetime total, including starting coins and soil rewards, so a near-limit save with prior spending cannot generate an unloadable candidate even when its wallet and ticket ledger separately remain safe.

Sale, payment status and pending income are one pure candidate. Duplicate payments cannot find the same visitor unpaid. Collection changes only coins/pending and becomes a no-op at zero pending. The unchanged committer freezes the candidate, blocks other mutation after failure, and publishes only after successful storage. Repeated retry does not rerun the sale or migration. Unsupported/corrupt/unavailable storage remains protected; only absent storage creates a fresh game.

## Automated coverage

- All nine unchanged Phase 7.1 fixtures through both migration and repeated schema-22 loading; complete field equality apart from the intended version/ledger and existing load-time recovery.
- Supported schemas 1–21, strict historical readers, and mixed schema-21 paid visits with locked prices 2/3/4.
- Existing 0/1/2/3-display pricing, arrival intervals, attended service, pending income and collection.
- Controlled 4/10/17/99-display pricing/accounting inputs, every price 2–18, and mixed historical/new price records. These tests exercise pure pricing/payment/visitor validation without adding or mutating fossil definitions; they do not claim a full 17-exhibit game exists.
- Purchase/deposit and travel preservation, exact schema-22 reloads, immutable failed-payment/collection/migration retry, duplicate attempts and reset.
- Malformed ledger data, canonical-key aliases, invalid counts/prices, overflow, sequence/queue/paid-history contradictions, corrupt finances/receipts/custody/progression, and a valid near-limit save whose next ticket must be refused.

`scripts/check-ticket-ledger.mjs` adds ten production-browser cases: nine frozen fixture migrations/reloads and a schema-21 migration write failure followed by duplicate retry. Each uses a disposable Edge context, frozen simulation and synthetic storage. A pre-economy fixture naturally receives a new seed; its progress/entitlements and zero retroactive rewards are checked independently.

## Independent engineering assessment

- **Verified:** The three-tier restriction is removed from normal-play accounting. The representation is bounded by safe-integer currency and earned progress, not a fixed number of prices. Existing pricing, intervals and interactions remain intact in unit and browser checks. All frozen source records migrate without granting currency; load recovery remains intentional and separate from parsing.
- **High — existing architectural risk, recommendation before Phase 7.3:** Historical museum/site/soil/upgrade validators still use live content definitions, as identified in Phase 7.1. Adding offers, changing costs, layouts, depths or fossil IDs can break older saves even though ticket accounting is now extensible. Freeze or explicitly version/map those contracts before altering persisted content. This task does not claim to solve that broader migration problem.
- **Medium — existing validator assumption:** Sales are constrained by the *current* completed-display price because display completion/ownership is presently permanent. A future redesign that removes or redistributes completed displays must preserve the historical earned tier or migrate the rule explicitly; otherwise valid old ticket history may be rejected. No such gameplay change is implemented here.
- **Medium — coverage limit:** Future high prices are verified through controlled pricing/accounting/visitor inputs, not through nonexistent 17-display museum definitions. When actual Phase 7 content arrives, add full-save and browser progression tests against the same ledger, including its interaction with new room/stand requirements.
- **Low — local persistence boundary:** These checks establish internal consistency and atomic retry semantics, not protection against deliberate, internally consistent save rewriting or browser data loss. Older application builds reject schema 22; there is no downgrade migration. The existing bundle-size warning remains outside this task.

No unresolved Critical defect was identified in the bounded ledger work. The safe-integer payment defect found during implementation was reproduced, fixed and covered by a regression test. West-wing credit migration remains a separate task; no access/credit policy is inferred from this change.
